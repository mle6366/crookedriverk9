Common Questions

What Vaccinations Are Required?

For all training programs & consultations we require proof that the dog is up to date on the Rabies (if 12 weeks or older), Distemper (DAPP), Leptospirosis, Bordetella, and Canine Influenza vaccines. Proof is required prior to the program / reservation start date.

Do You Offer a Dog Training Guarantee?

Dogs are living, breathing creatures with individual personalities, strengths and weaknesses, just like people. We are confident that if you consistently follow our training guidance and complete the recommended practice homework between sessions, you will see meaningful progress with your dog.


Because your dog's success depends on what happens outside of our training sessions—including consistency at home, proper use of training equipment, following the recommended techniques, and managing your dog's environment—we cannot guarantee specific outcomes in every situation.


For this reason, all training programs are non-refundable, and we do not offer a money-back guarantee.

What is the Purpose of a Prong Collar?

A prong collar is an effective training tool to communicate with your pup on-leash. The collar's design is made so pressure is evenly distributed around the neck, rather than targeting one specific area. Some people may be skeptical of prongs, but with the correct use and the right quality, the prong collar is a safe & harmless tool that will actually help build engagement between you & your pup. A prong collar is only meant for quick, short corrections in order to regain a dog's attention and help them understand the difference between what behaviors they should & shouldn't engage in. We carry only the highest quality prong collars and all of our trainers are skilled in properly fitting & using the tool. They will teach you how to properly use and put on the prong collar before sending you home with one.


Before trying out a prong collar as a training tool for your dog, we typically try various other training tools first (regular flat collar, slip collar, slip lead, starmark collar). If those don't seem to be a good fit for your pup, we will then try out a prong collar instead. If you choose to purchase one from somewhere else, we only recommend high quality prong collars (Herm Sprenger) as their design is the most reliable in safely regaining a dog’s attention as well as keeping the integrity of the product.

How Does E-Collar training work in Complete?

We use the e-collar as a communication tool — low-level, thoughtful conditioning paired with the commands your dog already understands. We slowly introduce the collar by having them wear it until they are comfortable with it, and then we introduce low level stims until we find that the dog has a response to it. We are looking for them to notice the stimulation but not be worried about it or have any adverse reaction to it.

What Does an E-Collar Feel Like?

An e-collar—short for electronic collar—is a wonderful communication tool for advancing and building on obedience as well as off leash reliability. High quality e-collars use TENS units, which in effect is a muscle stimulator. At a low to mid working level, it can feel like a pulsing or tingling sensation. A dog's working level is found by slowly introducing the collar by having them wear it until they are comfortable with it and then introducing low level stims until we find that the dog has a response to it. We are looking for them to notice the stimulation but not be worried about it or have any adverse reaction to it. These “working levels” can vary around lower and higher distractions, but can ultimately remain around the same.


Using this method is a great way to introduce the e-collar in a positive association and will not hurt your dog. This tool can easily be misused if not under the guidance of a professional dog trainer or can be introduced improperly. We only use high quality e-collars under our specific brand (Mini Educator). We will go over their use in more detail during the consultation and have you wear the e-collar on your hand so you can feel the sensation for yourself and learn more about this versatile tool.

How Do I Learn to Train My Dog After Graduation?

Graduation is just the beginning! Once your dog completes their training program, it's time for the most important phase—teaching you how to maintain and build on your dog's new skills. We'll provide you with detailed written guides and instructional videos covering how to reinforce your dog's training, properly use the training equipment, and handle situations when your dog inevitably tests the rules. (Don't worry—this is a normal part of the learning process!)


For the first two weeks after your dog comes home, we recommend keeping training sessions and outings in low-distraction environments. This allows you to build confidence, learn to read your dog's behavior, and develop the skills to proactively guide them through new situations before increasing the level of difficulty.


After those initial two weeks, we'll schedule a private follow-up lesson where we'll evaluate your handling skills and your dog's progress in more challenging, real-world environments. Our goal isn't just to train your dog—we're committed to training you as well. A well-trained dog is only successful when they have a knowledgeable, consistent handler, and we're here to help you become one.

What Is Your Method of Training?

We utilize a balanced training approach that combines positive reinforcement with clear, consistent accountability. Every dog begins their training journey by learning through rewards, shaping, and luring techniques that help build understanding, confidence, and engagement.


Once a dog has a solid grasp of the desired behaviors, we introduce fair and appropriate consequences to reinforce reliability and compliance. This helps dogs understand not only what is expected of them, but also that those expectations apply in real-world situations.

Our balanced training methodology produces dogs that are happy, motivated, and enthusiastic about working with their handlers while also understanding boundaries and responding reliably when it matters most.
